Active and Passive Voice Transformation Rules

Complete tense conversion formulas, modal verbs, imperative sentences & prepositional exceptions.

In Active Voice, the grammatical subject acts as the agent performing the action. In Passive Voice, the grammatical subject receives the action. Passive voice is preferred in official reports, legal documentation, and academic writing when the receiver or action is more prominent than the performer.

Universal Passive Transformation Formula: [Object of Active] + [Appropriate Form of 'To Be' (is/am/are/was/were/being/been)] + [Past Participle (V3)] + [Preposition (by/to/with/at)] + [Subject of Active in Objective Case]

Non-Transformable Tenses & Verbs

Imperative Sentences (Commands & Advice)

  • Orders/Commands: Let + Object + be + V3 (e.g. "Post this letter." → "Let this letter be posted.")
  • Moral Advice / Duty: Object + should be + V3 (e.g. "Respect your elders." → "Your elders should be respected.")
  • Polite Requests: You are requested to + V1 (e.g. "Please help me." → "You are requested to help me.")
  • Negative Commands: Let + Object + not + be + V3 (e.g. "Do not insult the poor." → "Let the poor not be insulted.")

Verbs Taking Prepositions Other Than 'By' (High-Frequency Exam Traps)

  • Known to: "I know him." → "He is known to me." (Never known by me!)
  • Surprised at: "Her behavior surprised us." → "We were surprised at her behavior."
  • Pleased with: "His results pleased his parents." → "His parents were pleased with his results."
  • Contained in: "This flask contains tea." → "Tea is contained in this flask."
  • Married to: "He married her." → "She was married to him."
  • Satisfied with: "The manager was satisfied with my performance."

Direct and Indirect Narration (Reported Speech) Rules

The 5 golden rules: pronoun conversion (S.O.N.), tense backshifting, conjunctions & universal truths.

Direct Speech quotes the actual spoken words enclosed in inverted commas (e.g. She said, "I am studying."). Indirect Speech reports the statement without verbatim quotation (e.g. She said that she was studying.).

Rule of Pronouns: The S.O.N. Formula

  • 1st Person Pronouns (I, we, me, us, my, our) change according to the Subject of the reporting verb.
  • 2nd Person Pronouns (you, your) change according to the Object of the reporting verb.
  • 3rd Person Pronouns (he, she, it, they, his, her, their) undergo NO change.

Tense Backshift Rules (If Reporting Verb is in Past)

  • Present Simple (V1) → Past Simple (V2)
  • Present Continuous (is/am/are) → Past Continuous (was/were)
  • Present Perfect (has/have) → Past Perfect (had)
  • Past Simple (V2) → Past Perfect (had + V3)
  • Past Continuous (was/were) → Past Perfect Continuous (had been)
  • Will / Shall → Would / Should | Can → Could | May → Might
Critical Exceptions: When Tenses Do NOT Change:

1. Universal Truths & Scientific Laws: The teacher said, "The Earth moves around the Sun." → The teacher said that the Earth moves around the Sun.
2. Habitual Actions & Proverbs: He said, "Honesty is the best policy." → He said that honesty is the best policy.
3. Present/Future Reporting Verb: If the reporting verb is in the present (says, tells) or future (will say), the reported tense never changes! (e.g. He says, "I am fine." → He says that he is fine.)

Interrogative Sentences (Questions)

  • Reporting verb becomes asked or inquired.
  • For Yes/No questions, introduce if or whether.
  • For Wh-questions (Who, Where, Why, What), the wh-word itself acts as the connective.
  • The sentence word order changes into assertive (Subject comes before Verb); question mark is dropped!

Optative & Exclamatory Sentences

  • Optative (Wishes/Prayers): Said → wished or prayed that. 'May' changes to 'might'. (e.g. He said, "May God bless you!" → He prayed that God might bless me.)
  • Exclamatory: Said + Alas! → exclaimed with sorrow that. Said + Hurrah! → exclaimed with joy that. Said + Wow! → exclaimed with wonder that.

Prepositions: Spatial, Temporal & Fixed Collocations

Prepositions of Time and Place, confusing pairs (between vs. among, beside vs. besides), and 60+ high-yield fixed prepositions.

Prepositions of Time: AT, ON, IN

  • AT: Precise clock times, dawn, noon, midnight, night (e.g. at 6:00 PM, at midnight, at dawn).
  • ON: Specific days and calendar dates (e.g. on Monday, on 14th August, on Friday morning).
  • IN: Months, years, seasons, centuries, long durations (e.g. in October, in 2026, in summer, in the morning).

Confusing Preposition Pairs Clarified

  • Between vs. Among: Between is strictly for two entities (or distinct mutual ties); Among is for more than two entities.
  • Beside vs. Besides: Beside = by the side of; Besides = in addition to / apart from.
  • Since vs. For: Since denotes a specific starting point in time; For denotes duration / length of time.
  • Die of vs. Die from: Die of a disease (cancer, malaria); Die from an external cause or wound (overwork, thirst).

The 8 Parts of Speech & Suffix Identification Clues

How to identify nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs in context without confusing morphology.

Noun Identification Clues

Nouns name persons, entities, concepts, or institutions. Look for these high-frequency derivational suffixes:

  • -tion / -sion: legislation, decision, evaluation
  • -ment: development, governance, agreement
  • -ness: kindness, awareness, darkness
  • -ity / -ty: sovereignty, integrity, diversity
  • -ance / -ence: surveillance, resilience, emergence
  • -ship / -hood: leadership, citizenship, brotherhood
  • -ism: nationalism, patriotism, capitalism

Adjective Identification Clues

Adjectives modify, qualify, or describe nouns or pronouns:

  • -ful / -less: powerful, resourceful, hopeless
  • -able / -ible: sustainable, credible, viable
  • -ive: decisive, progressive, collective
  • -ous / -ious: hazardous, ambitious, rigorous
  • -al / -ial: constitutional, judicial, financial
  • -ic / -ical: economic, diplomatic, historical
  • Order of Adjectives (OSASCOMP): Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose

Verb Classification Clues

  • -ize / -ise: harmonize, institutionalize
  • -ify: rectify, clarify, intensify
  • -en: strengthen, broaden, weaken
  • -ate: formulate, negotiate, advocate
  • Transitive Verbs: Transfer action to a direct object (e.g. He solved the problem.)
  • Intransitive Verbs: Do not take a direct object (e.g. The eagle soared.)

Adverb Identification & The -LY Trap

  • Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs (e.g. spoke eloquently, exceptionally talented).
  • Most adverbs end in -ly (swiftly, diligently, profoundly).
  • CRITICAL EXAM TRAP: Words ending in -ly formed from nouns are ADJECTIVES, not adverbs! (e.g. friendly, lovely, cowardly, scholarly, brotherly, costly).
  • To express adverbial manner for these words, write: "in a cowardly manner" or "in a friendly way".
