Fraction to Percentage Fast Conversion Table
Convert standard fractions to exact percentages instantaneously to eliminate long divisions.
Formula: 1/2=50%, 1/3=33.33%, 1/4=25%, 1/5=20%, 1/6=16.67%, 1/7=14.28%, 1/8=12.5%, 1/9=11.11%, 1/11=9.09%, 1/12=8.33%, 1/16=6.25%
Example: What is 12.5% of 640? Instead of 0.125 × 640, compute 640 / 8 = 80.
Pro Tip: Remember that 1/7 = 14.28% and 2/7 = 28.57% (digits double: 14 -> 28).
The Commutative Percentage Trick (x% of y = y% of x)
Whenever calculating x% of y seems awkward, reverse the numbers: x% of y is mathematically identical to y% of x.
Formula: x% of y = (x × y) / 100 = (y × x) / 100 = y% of x
Example: Find 16% of 50. Swap it: 50% of 16 = half of 16 = 8!
Pro Tip: Extremely helpful when one number is 25, 50, 75, or 20.
The Mental Split Method (10%, 5%, 1% Building Blocks)
Break any percentage into 10% (move decimal 1 place left), 1% (move decimal 2 places left), and 5% (half of 10%).
Formula: Target% = a(10%) + b(5%) + c(1%)
Example: Find 36% of 450: 10% = 45 -> 30% = 135; 5% = 22.5; 1% = 4.5. Total = 135 + 22.5 + 4.5 = 162.
Pro Tip: For 98% of X, compute 100% - 2% (X - 2 × 1%).
Annual Depreciation / Compounded Decay
When a value depreciates by r% annually, multiply the current value by (1 - r/100) for each year.
Formula: Value after n years = P × (1 - r/100)^n
Example: A computer worth Rs. 100,000 depreciates by 10% annually. Value after 2 years: Year 1 = 100,000 - 10,000 = 90,000; Year 2 = 90,000 - 9,000 = Rs. 81,000.
Pro Tip: Do not subtract 20%! 10% depreciation for 2 years equals 19% total depreciation, leaving 81%.
Successive Percentage Changes (Net Change Formula)
When a quantity changes by a% and then by b%, the net percentage change is given by the universal formula.
Formula: Net % Change = a + b + (a × b) / 100
Example: If price increases by 20% and then decreases by 10%: Net = +20 - 10 + (20 × -10)/100 = 10 - 2 = +8% increase.
Pro Tip: Use (+) for increases and (-) for decreases/discounts.
Consumption Adjustment to Maintain Constant Budget
If price of a commodity increases by R%, consumption must decrease by a specific fraction to keep total expenditure constant.
Formula: Reduction in Consumption % = [R / (100 + R)] × 100%
Example: If petrol price increases by 25%: Reduction = [25 / (100 + 25)] × 100% = (25/125) × 100% = 20% reduction.
Pro Tip: If price decreases by R%, consumption can increase by [R / (100 - R)] × 100%.

Multiplying Any Number by 11
Keep the outer digits identical and sum adjacent digits from right to left.
Formula: For ab × 11: write 'a', then (a+b), then 'b'. If (a+b) >= 10, carry over 1 to 'a'.
Example: 52 × 11: outer digits 5 and 2; middle = 5+2 = 7 -> 572. 87 × 11: 8 [8+7] 7 = 8 [15] 7 = (8+1) 5 7 = 957.
Pro Tip: For 3 digits: 342 × 11 -> First digit 3, then (3+4)=7, then (4+2)=6, last digit 2 -> 3,762.
Squaring Numbers Ending in 5
Multiply the leading number by its consecutive integer (N × (N+1)) and append 25 to the end.
Formula: (N5)^2 = [N × (N + 1)] 25
Example: 65^2: Leading is 6. 6 × 7 = 42. Append 25 -> 4,225! 85^2: 8 × 9 = 72 -> 7,225.
Pro Tip: Works for 3-digit numbers too: 115^2 -> 11 × 12 = 132 -> 13,225.
Multiplying Numbers Near Base 100
Find the deviation of each number from 100. Left part = (Num1 + Dev2). Right part = (Dev1 × Dev2) in 2 digits.
Formula: (100 + a)(100 + b) = [100 + a + b] | (a × b)
Example: 104 × 107: Devs are +4 and +7. Left = 104 + 7 = 111. Right = 4 × 7 = 28 -> 11,128! For below 100: 96 × 93 -> Devs -4 and -7. Left = 96 - 7 = 89. Right = (-4) × (-7) = 28 -> 8,928.
Pro Tip: Ensure the right-hand part always has 2 digits (e.g., 2 × 3 = 06).
Product of Numbers with Difference of 2 (a^2 - 1)
When two numbers differ by 2, their product is the square of the middle number minus 1.
Formula: (x - 1)(x + 1) = x^2 - 1
Example: 29 × 31: Middle number is 30. 30^2 - 1 = 900 - 1 = 899. 49 × 51 = 50^2 - 1 = 2,499.
Pro Tip: For difference of 4: (x - 2)(x + 2) = x^2 - 4. E.g., 28 × 32 = 30^2 - 4 = 896.
Multiplication by 25, 50, and 125
Instead of multiplying, divide by the reciprocal fraction and add zeros.
Formula: × 25 = (÷ 4) × 100; × 50 = (÷ 2) × 100; × 125 = (÷ 8) × 1000
Example: 64 × 25: 64 ÷ 4 = 16 -> 1,600. 72 × 125: 72 ÷ 8 = 9 -> 9,000.
Pro Tip: Faster than standard column multiplication by a factor of four.

Master Divisibility Test for 3 and 9
A number is divisible by 3 (or 9) if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3 (or 9).
Formula: Sum(digits) mod 3 = 0 => Divisible by 3; Sum(digits) mod 9 = 0 => Divisible by 9
Example: Is 48,726 divisible by 9? Sum = 4+8+7+2+6 = 27. 27 is divisible by 9, so YES.
Pro Tip: 'Cast out nines': cancel out any digits that add to 9 as you go to calculate faster.
Divisibility Test for 4 and 8
For 4: check only the last 2 digits. For 8: check only the last 3 digits.
Formula: Last 2 digits divisible by 4 => Num div by 4; Last 3 digits div by 8 => Num div by 8
Example: 753,824: Last 2 digits = 24 (div by 4 -> YES). Last 3 digits = 824 (824 ÷ 8 = 103 -> YES, div by 8).
Pro Tip: For 8: If the hundreds digit is even, check if last 2 digits are divisible by 8. If odd, add 4 to last 2 digits.
Alternating Sum Divisibility Test for 11
Take the difference between the sum of digits at odd places and sum of digits at even places. If the difference is 0 or divisible by 11, the number is divisible by 11.
Formula: |Sum(odd places) - Sum(even places)| = 0, 11, 22...
Example: Test 9,183,724: Odd places (from right): 4+7+8+9 = 28. Even places: 2+3+1 = 6. Difference = 28 - 6 = 22 (divisible by 11 -> YES).
Pro Tip: Extremely common in FPSC, CSS, and PPSC aptitude tests.
Fast Division by 5 and 50
To divide by 5, double the number and shift the decimal one place left. To divide by 50, double and shift two places left.
Formula: X / 5 = (2X) / 10; X / 50 = (2X) / 100
Example: 342 ÷ 5: Double 342 = 684. Shift decimal -> 68.4. 1,450 ÷ 50: Double = 2,900. Shift two decimals -> 29.
Pro Tip: To divide by 25: Multiply by 4 and shift two decimals left.
Converting Recurring Decimals to Fractions
Pure recurring decimals have 9s in the denominator. Mixed recurring have 9s followed by 0s.
Formula: 0.aaaa... = a/9; 0.abab... = ab/99; 0.ab_bar = (ab - a) / 90
Example: 0.4777... = (47 - 4) / 90 = 43/90. 0.2323... = 23/99.
Pro Tip: Number of 9s = count of repeating digits; number of 0s = count of non-repeating digits after decimal.

The 'N-Method' to Combine Two Ratios (A:B and B:C -> A:B:C)
Multiply vertically and diagonally to find the unified ratio without finding common multiples.
Formula: A : B : C = (A × B2) : (B1 × B2) : (B1 × C)
Example: If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. A = 2×4 = 8; B = 3×4 = 12; C = 3×5 = 15. A:B:C = 8:12:15.
Pro Tip: Keep A:B on top and B:C on bottom aligned at B.
Combining 4 Ratios (A:B:C:D)
Align ratios in a table and fill empty spaces with the adjacent number.
Formula: Row 1: [A, B, B, B]; Row 2: [A, B, C, C]; Row 3: [A, B, C, D] -> multiply columns.
Example: If A:B = 1:2, B:C = 3:4, C:D = 5:6. A = 1×3×5 = 15; B = 2×3×5 = 30; C = 2×4×5 = 40; D = 2×4×6 = 48. Unified = 15:30:40:48.
Pro Tip: Simplify the final ratio by dividing by common factors if any.
Equivalent Ratio Property
Multiplying or dividing both antecedent and consequent by the same non-zero number produces an equivalent ratio.
Formula: a : b = ka : kb
Example: A ratio equivalent to 3:7 is (3×3) : (7×3) = 9:21 (FPSC Exam Question).
Pro Tip: Check each option by dividing both numbers by their GCD to see if it reduces to original.
Mixture Replacement Formula (Successive Dilution)
If a container has V liters of pure liquid and x liters are drawn out and replaced with water n times, the remaining pure liquid quantity is given by:
Formula: Remaining Liquid = Initial Volume × [1 - (x / V)]^n
Example: A vessel has 40L of milk. 4L are drawn and replaced with water, repeated twice (total 2 operations). Remaining = 40 × [1 - 4/40]^2 = 40 × (0.9)^2 = 40 × 0.81 = 32.4 Liters.
Pro Tip: Saves up to 3 minutes over step-by-step fractional calculation.

Two Workers Together Shortcut (Product over Sum)
If Person A completes work in A hours and Person B in B hours, working together they finish in (A × B) / (A + B) hours.
Formula: Time = (A × B) / (A + B)
Example: Mr. A completes work in 6 hours, Mr. B in 12 hours. Together: (6 × 12) / (6 + 12) = 72 / 18 = 4 hours! (FPSC Past Paper).
Pro Tip: Instant 5-second solution; avoids adding 1/6 + 1/12.
The Universal LCM (Unit Work) Method
Assume total work is the LCM of the individual times. Calculate daily/hourly units of work for each person.
Formula: Total Units = LCM(t1, t2, t3); Combined Daily Units = u1 + u2 + u3; Time = Total Units / Combined Units
Example: A does work in 10 days, B in 15 days, C in 30 days. LCM = 30 units. A = 3 units/day, B = 2 units/day, C = 1 unit/day. Combined = 3+2+1 = 6 units/day. Time = 30 / 6 = 5 days.
Pro Tip: Handles workers leaving early, alternate day working, and negative work (leaks) effortlessly.
Man-Days-Hours-Work Formula (MDH / W)
The relation between workers, days, daily hours, efficiency, and work done across two scenarios is constant.
Formula: (M1 × D1 × H1 × E1) / W1 = (M2 × D2 × H2 × E2) / W2
Example: If 15 men can build a 100m wall in 10 days working 8 hrs/day, how many days will 20 men take to build a 200m wall working 6 hrs/day? (15 × 10 × 8) / 100 = (20 × D2 × 6) / 200 => 12 = (120 × D2)/200 => D2 = 20 days.
Pro Tip: If work is identical, simply equate M1×D1×H1 = M2×D2×H2.
Pipes & Cisterns (Inlet and Leak Combined)
Inlets perform positive work; leaks/outlets perform negative work. If Inlet fills in A hours and Outlet empties in B hours (where B > A):
Formula: Net Time to Fill = (A × B) / (B - A)
Example: A pipe fills a tank in 6 hours, but a leak empties it in 10 hours. Time to fill: (6 × 10) / (10 - 6) = 60 / 4 = 15 hours.
Pro Tip: If tank is emptying, time = (A × B) / (A - B).

Instant Speed Unit Conversion (km/h <-> m/s)
To convert km/h to m/s multiply by 5/18. To convert m/s to km/h multiply by 18/5.
Formula: Speed(m/s) = Speed(km/h) × (5 / 18); Speed(km/h) = Speed(m/s) × (18 / 5)
Example: Convert 72 km/h to m/s: 72 × (5/18) = 4 × 5 = 20 m/s. Convert 25 m/s to km/h: 25 × (18/5) = 5 × 18 = 90 km/h.
Pro Tip: Notice that 18 km/h = 5 m/s, 36 km/h = 10 m/s, 54 km/h = 15 m/s, 72 km/h = 20 m/s.
Average Speed for Equal Distance (Harmonic Mean)
If a journey covers equal distance at speed x and returns at speed y, the average speed is not the arithmetic average.
Formula: Average Speed = (2 × x × y) / (x + y)
Example: A car travels from A to B at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. Avg Speed = (2 × 60 × 40) / (60 + 40) = 4,800 / 100 = 48 km/h (NOT 50 km/h!).
Pro Tip: For 3 equal distances: Avg Speed = 3xyz / (xy + yz + zx).
Train Crossing Relative Speed Rules
Total distance is always the sum of lengths. Speeds add if traveling in opposite directions; subtract if in same direction.
Formula: Opposite: S_rel = S1 + S2; Same: S_rel = |S1 - S2|; Time = (L_train1 + L_object) / S_rel
Example: A 150m train at 54 km/h crosses a pole: Dist = 150m, Speed = 54 × (5/18) = 15 m/s. Time = 150 / 15 = 10 seconds.
Pro Tip: For stationary objects with length (platform, bridge), Distance = Length(train) + Length(platform).
Boats and Streams (Upstream & Downstream)
Let Boat speed in still water = B, Stream speed = S. Downstream D = B + S. Upstream U = B - S.
Formula: Boat in Still Water B = (D + U) / 2; Stream Speed S = (D - U) / 2
Example: A boat moves at 14 km/h downstream and 8 km/h upstream. Boat speed = (14 + 8) / 2 = 11 km/h. Stream speed = (14 - 8) / 2 = 3 km/h.
Pro Tip: D is always greater than U.

The Multiplier Factor Method
Convert profit or loss into a direct multiplier to obtain Selling Price from Cost Price instantly.
Formula: Profit of p%: SP = CP × (1 + p/100); Loss of l%: SP = CP × (1 - l/100)
Example: An item costs Rs. 450. Find SP at 20% profit: SP = 450 × 1.2 = Rs. 540. At 15% loss: SP = 450 × 0.85 = Rs. 382.5.
Pro Tip: To find CP from SP: CP = SP / Multiplier.
Successive Discounts Shortcut
Two successive discounts of d1% and d2% are equivalent to a single net discount.
Formula: Single Equivalent Discount = d1 + d2 - (d1 × d2) / 100
Example: Successive discounts of 20% and 10%: Net Discount = 20 + 10 - (20 × 10)/100 = 30 - 2 = 28% (NOT 30%!).
Pro Tip: For three discounts: compute net of first two, then combine result with third.
Same SP with Equal % Profit and % Loss
When two articles are sold at the same price—one at x% profit and the other at x% loss—there is ALWAYS an overall net loss.
Formula: Net Loss % = (x / 10)^2 % = (x^2 / 100) %
Example: Two horses sold for Rs. 20,000 each; one at 20% gain, other at 20% loss. Net Result = Loss of (20^2 / 100)% = 400/100 = 4% loss.
Pro Tip: The selling price value does not affect the percentage loss result.
Dishonest Dealer (Cheating with Weights)
If a trader sells goods at cost price but uses a false weight:
Formula: Gain % = [Error / (True Weight - Error)] × 100%
Example: A shopkeeper sells sugar at cost price but uses 900g instead of 1kg (1000g). Error = 100g. Gain% = [100 / (1000 - 100)] × 100% = (100/900) × 100% = 11.11%.
Pro Tip: True Weight - Error is the actual weight handed to the customer.

Difference Between CI and SI for 2 Years
The difference between compound interest and simple interest over 2 years at annual rate r% on principal P:
Formula: Diff_2yr = P × (r / 100)^2
Example: P = Rs. 5,000, r = 10%, t = 2 years. Diff = 5,000 × (10/100)^2 = 5,000 × 0.01 = Rs. 50.
Pro Tip: Saves having to calculate both CI and SI separately and subtracting.
The Rule of 72 (Time to Double Money)
At r% compound interest, an investment doubles in approximately 72 / r years.
Formula: Doubling Years ≈ 72 / Annual Interest Rate (r)
Example: At 8% compound interest, money doubles in 72 / 8 = 9 years. At 12%, it doubles in 72 / 12 = 6 years.
Pro Tip: Rule of 114 gives tripling time; Rule of 144 gives quadrupling time.
Doubling Time for Simple Interest
Under simple interest, for a sum to double, the interest earned must equal the principal (SI = P).
Formula: Years to Double (t) = 100 / r; Rate to Double (r) = 100 / t
Example: At what SI rate will a sum double in 8 years? r = 100 / 8 = 12.5%.
Pro Tip: To triple under SI: t = 200 / r. To n-fold under SI: t = (n - 1) × 100 / r.

The Assumed Mean / Deviation Method
Pick a convenient assumed average A near the middle. Average = A + (Sum of Deviations / Count).
Formula: Average = A + [Sum(x_i - A) / n]
Example: Numbers: 182, 186, 188, 194, 190. Pick A = 190. Deviations: -8, -4, -2, +4, 0 = -10. Avg = 190 + (-10/5) = 190 - 2 = 188.
Pro Tip: Avoids adding large multi-digit numbers.
The Rule of Alligation (Cross Mixture Method)
Find the ratio in which two ingredients of different prices/qualities must be blended to produce a desired mixture price.
Formula: (Quantity of Cheaper) / (Quantity of Dearer) = (Dearer Price - Mean Price) / (Mean Price - Cheaper Price)
Example: In what ratio must rice at Rs. 60/kg be mixed with rice at Rs. 90/kg to sell at Rs. 72/kg? Cheaper = 60, Dearer = 90, Mean = 72. Cross differences: (90 - 72) = 18; (72 - 60) = 12. Ratio = 18 : 12 = 3 : 2.
Pro Tip: Always place Cheaper on left, Dearer on right, Mean in center.
New Member Joining Average Shift
When a new member joins an existing group of n people and increases average by x:
Formula: Weight/Age of New Person = Old Average + (n_new × Increase)
Example: Average weight of 19 students is 40 kg. With teacher included (n=20), average becomes 41 kg (+1 kg). Teacher's weight = 40 + (20 × 1) = 60 kg.
Pro Tip: If average decreases by x, use: Old Average - (n_new × Decrease).

Arithmetic Progression (AP) n-th Term
In an arithmetic progression with first term a and common difference d = (T_n - T_{n-1}):
Formula: T_n = a + (n - 1) × d
Example: If AP is -15, -22, -29, ..., find the 11th term: a = -15, d = -22 - (-15) = -7. T_11 = -15 + (11 - 1) × (-7) = -15 - 70 = -85 (FPSC Past Paper).
Pro Tip: Be vigilant with negative common differences.
Arithmetic Mean (AM) Properties
A single AM between two numbers a and b is (a + b) / 2. The sum of n AMs between a and b is n × [(a + b) / 2].
Formula: Single AM = (a + b) / 2; Sum of n AMs = n × [(a + b) / 2]
Example: Sum of 4 AMs between 18 and 3: 4 × [(18 + 3) / 2] = 4 × 10.5 = 42 (FPSC Past Paper). Single AM between (x-3) and (x+3) = [(x-3) + (x+3)] / 2 = 2x / 2 = x (FPSC Past Paper).
Pro Tip: Sum of any number of AMs is simply n times the average of the two extremes.
Sum of First n Natural, Even, and Odd Numbers
Standard closed formulas to calculate series sums in 3 seconds.
Formula: Natural: n(n + 1) / 2; Even: n(n + 1); Odd: n^2; Squares: n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6
Example: Sum of first 20 odd numbers = 20^2 = 400. Sum of first 50 natural numbers = 50 × 51 / 2 = 1,275.
Pro Tip: Number of odd numbers from 1 to L is (L + 1) / 2.

BODMAS Order of Operations
Operations must be performed strictly from left to right in order: Brackets -> Orders (Powers/Roots) -> Division & Multiplication -> Addition & Subtraction.
Formula: B -> O -> D -> M -> A -> S
Example: Evaluate 27 ÷ 32 ÷ 4 × 2 - 1 or 24 ÷ 6 × 2 + 5: 24 ÷ 6 = 4 -> 4 × 2 = 8 -> 8 + 5 = 13.
Pro Tip: Consecutive divisions evaluate strictly from left to right: a ÷ b ÷ c = (a / b) / c = a / (b × c).
Solving Linear Inequalities
When multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.
Formula: ax + b >= c => ax >= c - b => x >= (c - b) / a (if a > 0)
Example: What is the solution interval of 6x - 2 >= 3x + 10? 6x - 3x >= 10 + 2 => 3x >= 12 => x >= 4. Interval = [4, ∞) (FPSC Past Paper).
Pro Tip: Use square bracket [ for >= or <= and round parenthesis ( for > or <.
Three-Set Venn Diagram Formula (Inclusion-Exclusion)
To find the number of elements belonging to at least one set among three overlapping categories:
Formula: n(A ∪ B ∪ C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) - [n(A∩B) + n(B∩C) + n(A∩C)] + n(A∩B∩C)
Example: In a town: 45% read magazine A, 55% B, 40% C. 30% read A&B, 15% B&C, 25% A&C. 10% read all three. Percentage reading NONE: n(A∪B∪C) = 45+55+40 - (30+15+25) + 10 = 140 - 70 + 10 = 80%. None = 100% - 80% = 20%! (FPSC Past Paper).
Pro Tip: Add individuals, subtract pair-wise intersections, add back the three-way intersection.
Age Word Problem Formulation
Express time-shifted statements with parentheses before scaling.
Formula: n times age k years ago = n × (x - k); n times age k years hence = n × (x + k)
Example: If Amna is x years old then 5 times Amna's age three years ago is: 5(x - 3) (FPSC Past Paper).
Pro Tip: Do not write 5x - 3; the subtraction happens before the multiplication.

Number of Diagonals in an n-Sided Polygon
The total number of diagonals in a regular or irregular polygon with n vertices:
Formula: Diagonals = [n × (n - 3)] / 2

Handshakes / Match Matches Formula
If n persons in a room shake hands with each other exactly once, the total number of handshakes is:
Formula: Total Handshakes = [n × (n - 1)] / 2
Example: 12 people attend a meeting and each shakes hands with everyone else. Handshakes = (12 × 11) / 2 = 66.
Pro Tip: Identical to round-robin tournament matches among n teams.
The Complementary Probability Trick (At Least One)
Whenever asked to find the probability of 'at least one' occurrence, calculate 1 minus the probability of NONE.
Formula: P(At least 1 success) = 1 - P(Zero successes)
Example: A coin is tossed 3 times. What is the probability of getting at least one Head? P(Zero heads / all tails) = (1/2)^3 = 1/8. P(At least 1 Head) = 1 - 1/8 = 7/8.
Pro Tip: Reduces multiple case computations to a single fraction.
Circular Arrangements
The number of ways to arrange n distinct objects around a circular table is (n - 1)!.
Formula: Circular Permutations = (n - 1)!
Example: In how many ways can 5 people be seated at a round table? (5 - 1)! = 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24 ways.
Pro Tip: For necklaces or garlands where clockwise/anticlockwise are identical, divide by 2: (n - 1)! / 2.
